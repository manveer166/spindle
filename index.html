Spindle privacy policy

This covers Spindle Pod on Android and Spindle on iPhone. Some sections describe features only one of them has.

Effective 25 November 2026

Spindle Pod does not collect, store or share any personal data. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.

Music

Spindle reads the music files already on your phone to build its library and play them. Your music and your library stay on your phone.

Google Drive

If you add songs from Google Drive, you choose them in Drive's own picker, and Android lets Spindle read only those files. Spindle streams each song from Drive when you play it and keeps the song's name, tags and a small cover image on your phone. It never uploads, changes or deletes anything in your Drive. Files go between your phone and Google; Google's privacy policy covers Drive.

Tags in your files

Ratings, BPM, genres and tags you set are kept on your phone. If you choose Write tag changes, Android asks your permission and Spindle writes them into those music files on your phone. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Ask

Ask reads your request on the phone. On phones that have Google's Gemini Nano, Ask may pass the request, and the names of the genres in your library, to Gemini Nano, which runs on the phone through Android's AICore service; that stays on the phone. Only if you add your own Gemini API key, and Gemini Nano isn't ready, Ask sends the request and those genre names to Google's Gemini API, under Google's terms. The key is kept on your phone.

Internet radio

To list stations, Spindle asks the Radio Browser directory (radio-browser.info), a free community service, for stations by name, country or genre, and tells it which station you started, which is how it ranks stations. The stream itself comes straight from the station. Your favourite and recent stations are kept on your phone.

Media servers

If you add a Plex, Jellyfin, Emby or Subsonic server, Spindle signs in to it with the address, user name and password you enter (for Plex, through plex.tv), and keeps the sign-in token on your phone to list and play your music. For Plex, Jellyfin and Emby your password isn't stored. Subsonic and Navidrome need it for every request, so for those it is kept in Spindle's private storage on your phone, which other apps can't read. Nothing goes anywhere other than the server you added. Removing a server deletes its token and cached art.

Android Auto

When you connect to a car, Android Auto reads your album list, song names and covers from Spindle to show them on the car's screen. That happens on your phone and in your car; Spindle sends nothing elsewhere.

Listen together

If you host or join a Listen together session, Spindle connects directly to phones nearby over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, using Google Play services' Nearby Connections, with no server in between. The phones exchange the name you choose to show, what's playing, the queue, and pause or skip requests. When someone sends a song, or the host has "Everyone hears it" on, the song file is copied to the other phone's cache for the session and deleted when the session ends. Android asks for the Nearby devices permission for this; on Android 12 and older it calls this a location permission, but Spindle never reads your location.

Car mode

Car mode reads song and station names aloud with the phone's text-to-speech and keeps voice commands listening, as described under Microphone. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Camera

If you turn on hand gestures, eye control, mouth to lock or the camera view, Spindle uses the front camera while it is on screen. Each frame is analysed on the phone to find the position of your hand and face, then discarded. No picture or video is saved, shown or sent anywhere. The camera view and the tutorial draw outlines only. If you record face shortcuts, Spindle keeps only a short list of numbers for each one (how much each part of your face moved, such as a smile or raised brows), stored on your phone. No picture of your face is kept, and deleting a shortcut deletes its numbers.

Microphone

If you turn on voice commands, Spindle listens for commands while it is on screen. On Android 13 and later it uses the phone's on-device speech recogniser when one is installed. Otherwise, Android's speech service may process the audio under Google's privacy policy. The visualisers use the microphone permission only to read the sound Spindle itself is playing; they do not record.

Settings

Your settings and playlists are stored on your phone and are deleted when you uninstall Spindle.
